        Tips
        
    
          
  • Use a plain cloth or board whose color is not in the puzzle (e.g. black felt for a bright puzzle).
    • 
          
  • Pale or white pieces: lay them on a dark cloth or towel. On a white board their outlines get cut short (in testing, 9% of pale pieces read right on white vs all of them on dark). With a glass table, a tablet or laptop showing a plain white screen underneath works too: the pieces show up as sharp silhouettes.
    • 
          
  • Glare (shiny pieces, a glass table): light from the side instead of overhead, or tilt the phone a little — the tilt is corrected. A sheet of polarizing film over the lamp plus one over the camera removes most reflections.
    • 
          
  • Pieces face up, not touching.
    • 
          
  • Light: a lamp off to one side, low over the table, makes every piece cast a thin shadow, which helps separate pieces from a cloth of similar color. Avoid a strong light directly overhead that bleaches the print.
    • 
          
  • Hold the phone flat, 30–40 cm above the table, and sweep slowly. Pause a moment over each area; outlines turn blue then green as pieces are read.
    • 
          
  • Border (toolbar) lights up the whole frame of the puzzle at once — corners orange, edge pieces teal. Tap it again to turn it off.
    • 
          
  • Catalog from a photo (below) takes a full-resolution photo and catalogs every piece in it at once.
    • 
          
  • Tap a piece to see where it goes on the box and which pieces fit it. Gold = best match.
    • 
          
  • Drag across the box picture to light up every piece from that area. Tap it to enlarge it; on the enlarged picture, tap one spot to see the loose pieces that best fill it — ranked by the picture and by how well they fit the pieces already around that spot (gold on the table).
    • 
          
  • In Find, tap Corners or Edges to light up every border piece on the table — orange for corners, teal for edges. Arrows point to the ones just off screen.
    • 
          
  • Matches searches the whole catalog for pairs that fit, with no box picture needed; use ‹ › to step through them.
    • 
          
  • Check the matches: try each suggested pair and tap Fits or No (in Matches or in a piece's panel). The app keeps score — More shows how often it was right — and Send report shares the answers so the matching can be tuned. A quick way: separate pieces you have already fitted together, scan them, then judge the suggestions.
    • 
          
  • A piece is catalogued only after the camera has seen it clearly a few frames in a row, and its shape counts as confirmed once a second, later view agrees. Matches between confirmed shapes can turn gold; a dashed frame or "?" means a shape read only once.
    • 
          
  • Box picture (in Find) hides or shows the small box picture when it's in the way.
    • 
          
  • The white arrow on a selected piece points to its top edge as it sits in the puzzle; the panel also shows it upright.
    • 
          
  • Zones (in Find) splits the box picture into six areas, A–F, each in its own colour, and rings every piece on the table in the colour of its area: sort them into six trays. The small box picture shows which area is which.
    • 
          
  • Mark as in the puzzle (in a piece's panel) once it's placed: its spot on the box is no longer offered for other pieces, and Border and the finders skip it. Tap again to undo.
    • 
          
  • Matches are labelled Strong (very likely, and backed by a second check — the two pieces pick each other, or a 2×2 block closes), Likely, Maybe or Look-alike (another piece is about as good). The percentages learn from your Fits / No answers.
    • 
          
  • Map turns the camera off and shows the scanned pieces from above, as their own pictures, so several people can work from one screen. Drag to move, pinch to zoom, twist (or ↻ 90°) to turn it toward you, tap a piece for its matches. The finders and Matches work here too. Pieces moved since scanning: switch to Scan and sweep that area again.
